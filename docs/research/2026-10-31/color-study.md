## 1. What apps reserve for reward
- Duolingo: Feather Green #58CC02 is the core colour, Macaw #1CB0F6 secondary (https://design.duolingo.com/identity/color, per search summary, page not read directly). Streak orange #FF9600 is unofficial (open-design.ai). No official "why" found.
- Apple rings: Move red, Exercise green, Stand blue; one colour per goal, closing the ring is the reward (https://support.apple.com/HT205406, via search summary).
- Whoop: Recovery banded green ~67-100, yellow ~34-66, red ~1-33 (https://www.whoop.com/au/en/thelocker/how-does-whoop-recovery-work-101/, via search summary). Traffic light, status by colour, which Edwin's rule rejects.
- Oura: "Contributors where you receive a 'pay attention' message with a red progress bar are areas for potential improvement." and "85 or higher: Optimal" (https://ouraring.com/blog/readiness-score/, fetched 31 Oct 2026).
- Gentler Streak: "No guilt, no punishment." and "Streaks that celebrate rest." (https://gentler.app/, fetched 31 Oct 2026). No colour rationale stated.
- MacroFactor: "You'll never see warnings, red numbers, or shaming when you go over your calorie or macro targets." (App Store listing https://apps.apple.com/app/id1553503471, 31 Oct 2026; company claim).
- Strava, Headspace, Finch: unchecked.

## 2. Evidence on colour and motivation
- Red as avoidance cue (Elliot et al. 2007, https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2007/02/070228170240.htm): red before a test impaired performance. WEAK: replications failed (Steele posters, https://www.appstate.edu/~steelekm/documents/Psychonomics2015Poster_Steele.pdf) and two meta-analyses disagree (https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13423-020-01772-1, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7704521/). Only the test-performance context, not calorie apps.
- NN/g (https://www.nngroup.com/articles/color-enhance-design/, 6 Jun 2021) is general colour theory; nothing on reward or food.
- Red/green for food, shame colours: NO evidence found. The no-red-over-target rule rests on MacroFactor's product choice and Gentler's stance, both design opinion. Also red/green fails for colour-blind users; Edwin's shape-not-colour rule covers that (my reasoning).
